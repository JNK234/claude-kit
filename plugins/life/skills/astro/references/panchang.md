## 1. The 30 Tithis

A tithi is the time it takes the Moon to gain 12° on the Sun. There are 30 tithis in a lunar month, split into two pakshas:

- **Shukla Paksha** (waxing, bright fortnight) — Pratipada through Purnima (full moon)
- **Krishna Paksha** (waning, dark fortnight) — Pratipada through Amavasya (new moon)

The fifteen names (used in both pakshas):
1. Pratipada (1st) — beginnings, foundation work
2. Dwitiya (2nd) — relationship, agreements
3. Tritiya (3rd) — strengthening, exercise, education
4. Chaturthi (4th) — Ganesha's day; remove obstacles. Sankashti Chaturthi (Krishna paksha) and Vinayaka Chaturthi (Shukla paksha) are sacred
5. Panchami (5th) — knowledge, naga puja
6. Shashthi (6th) — Skanda/Kartikeya; childcare, healing
7. Saptami (7th) — Surya; health, vitality
8. Ashtami (8th) — Durga; energy intense, can be challenging
9. Navami (9th) — Durga, Rama; spiritual power
10. Dashami (10th) — completion, dharma
11. Ekadashi (11th) — fasting day; spiritual practices, NOT good for material starts
12. Dwadashi (12th) — break the Ekadashi fast (parana); travel
13. Trayodashi (13th) — Pradosh; Shiva worship at twilight
14. Chaturdashi (14th) — Krishna paksha 14 = Shivaratri; intense; not for new ventures
15. Purnima (Shukla 15) / Amavasya (Krishna 15) — Full moon: completion, lakshmi puja. New moon: ancestor (pitru) work, introspection

### Tithi categories (general guidance)
- **Nanda** (1, 6, 11) — joyous, good for celebrations
- **Bhadra** (2, 7, 12) — prosperity-oriented
- **Jaya** (3, 8, 13) — victory-oriented, good for competition
- **Rikta** (4, 9, 14) — empty/draining; avoid major auspicious work
- **Purna** (5, 10, 15) — fullness, good for completion

### Special nakshatra groupings
- **Gandanta nakshatras** — junctions of water-fire signs: end of Revati / start of Ashwini, end of Ashlesha / start of Magha, end of Jyeshtha / start of Mula. Births in these need extra care.
- **Mool nakshatras** — Ashwini, Ashlesha, Magha, Jyeshtha, Mula, Revati. Birth in these traditionally calls for shanti puja.
- **Gentle (Mridu)** — Mrigashira, Chitra, Anuradha, Revati.
- **Sharp (Tikshna)** — Ardra, Ashlesha, Jyeshtha, Mula.

## 3. The 27 Yogas

A Yoga is a specific Sun-Moon angular relationship (each yoga = 13°20' of combined Sun+Moon longitude). Some are auspicious, some not.

The 27 yogas in order:
1. Vishkambha 2. Priti 3. Ayushman 4. Saubhagya 5. Shobhana 6. Atiganda* 7. Sukarma 8. Dhriti 9. Shula* 10. Ganda* 11. Vriddhi 12. Dhruva 13. Vyaghata* 14. Harshana 15. Vajra* 16. Siddhi 17. Vyatipata* 18. Variyana 19. Parigha* 20. Shiva 21. Siddha 22. Sadhya 23. Shubha 24. Shukla 25. Brahma 26. Indra 27. Vaidhriti*

*Asterisked yogas are considered inauspicious (the "9 bad yogas") and major work is generally avoided during them.

## 4. The 11 Karanas

A karana is half a tithi. There are 11 named karanas — 7 movable (chara) that repeat 8 times in a month, and 4 fixed (sthira) that occur once each in the krishna paksha.

**Movable (cycle through the lunar month):**
1. Bava 2. Balava 3. Kaulava 4. Taitila 5. Gara 6. Vanija 7. **Vishti** (also called Bhadra — the inauspicious one; avoid major work)

**Fixed (once per month, at the very end and start):**
8. Shakuni 9. Chatushpada 10. Naga 11. Kimstughna

## 6. The 12 Masas (lunar months)

Two reckonings exist:
- **Amanta** — month ends at Amavasya (used in South India, Maharashtra, Gujarat)
- **Purnimanta** — month ends at Purnima (used in North India, Bihar, etc.)

Order: Chaitra → Vaishakha → Jyeshtha → Ashadha → Shravana → Bhadrapada → Ashwin → Kartika → Margashirsha (Agrahayana) → Pausha → Magha → Phalguna.

## 7. Calendar systems

- **Vikram Samvat** — starts from 57 BCE. To get year, add 57 to Gregorian year (approx; depends on which month).
- **Shaka Samvat** — starts from 78 CE. Subtract 78 from Gregorian year.
- **Kali Yuga** — started 3102 BCE per Surya Siddhanta tradition.

Each year also has a name in the 60-year Samvatsara cycle (e.g., Siddharthi, Parabhava — these rotate).

## 8. Auspicious & inauspicious time-windows

### Daily auspicious windows
- **Abhijit Muhurat** — ~24 minutes around solar noon. Universally good (except Wednesday by some traditions).
- **Brahma Muhurat** — ~1 hour 36 minutes before sunrise. Best for spiritual practice and meditation.
- **Godhuli** — twilight at sunset. Auspicious for marriages.

### Daily inauspicious windows
- **Rahu Kala** — ~90 minutes daily, varies by weekday:
  - Mon: 7:30–9:00 AM
  - Tue: 3:00–4:30 PM
  - Wed: 12:00–1:30 PM
  - Thu: 1:30–3:00 PM
  - Fri: 10:30 AM–12:00 PM
  - Sat: 9:00–10:30 AM
  - Sun: 4:30–6:00 PM
  (Approximate — actual depends on local sunrise/sunset; use drikpanchang.com for precise.)
- **Yamaganda** — another daily inauspicious window, varies by day.
- **Gulika Kala** — ruled by Saturn's son Gulika; avoid for new starts.
- **Dur Muhurat** — ~48-minute inauspicious window per day.
- **Varjyam** — daily ~96-minute window to avoid (popular in South India).

### Special auspicious yogas (combinations)
- **Sarvarthasiddhi Yoga** — specific weekday-nakshatra combinations that grant success in all undertakings.
- **Amritsiddhi Yoga** — another auspicious weekday-nakshatra combo.
- **Ravi Pushya / Guru Pushya** — Pushya nakshatra falling on Sunday or Thursday — extremely auspicious for buying gold, starting ventures.
- **Triphushkar / Dwipushkar Yoga** — multipliers; results triple or double.

### Avoid for new starts
- Vishti karana (Bhadra)
- Krishna paksha Chaturdashi (just before Amavasya)
- The 9 bad yogas (asterisked above)
- Rahu Kala, Yamaganda, Gulika Kala
- Eclipse periods (and 12 hours before for solar eclipse, 9 hours before for lunar eclipse — sutak)
- Panchak — when Moon is in last 5 nakshatras (Dhanishtha 2nd half, Shatabhisha, Purva Bhadrapada, Uttara Bhadrapada, Revati). Specifically avoid: travel south, fuel/wood purchase, building bed, cremation rites.
