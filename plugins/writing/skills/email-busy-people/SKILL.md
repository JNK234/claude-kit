# Email to Busy People — The Outreach Method

This is the canonical outreach method: the methodology (what to do before writing), the voice (how to write), and the follow-up cadence (what to do after). `find-and-reach` finds + verifies the person; THIS skill governs the message and the follow-through.

## Core Principle

**Busy people skim. Optimize for "easy to skim, easy to respond, easy to forward."**

A great email here = the recipient can read it in 15 seconds and know exactly what action to take, with zero friction to reply or forward.

## The 8 Rules (outreach doctrine — apply to every message)

1. **Target people, not portals.** Find the named researcher / team lead / hiring manager who owns the problem. Job portals are a parallel track, never the main one.
2. **Open with their work, not yourself.** First sentence proves you read their paper / used their tool / understood their problem. No intros, no "hope this finds you well," no fan language.
3. **Lead with ONE artifact.** One link to your best work + one line of framing (what it does + why it's relevant to them). The artifact is the pitch; you are the footnote. One line of credential max.
4. **6–8 sentences.** If it scrolls on a phone, cut it. Short = competent; long = desperate.
5. **One small, specific ask.** "Is your team hiring for X this year?" / "Could you forward this to whoever owns research hiring?" / "15 min for one question about X?" NEVER "any opportunities available?" — vague asks die.
6. **Follow up on a fixed cadence.** No reply → one follow-up day 7–9 (two lines, re-link artifact, zero guilt). Alive-then-dead → one more touch 3–4 weeks later. Three touches max. Track every thread.
7. **Revive every dead thread.** Old recruiters, stalled processes, silent interviews → re-ping with one line of news ("since we spoke I built/wrote X — still very interested").
8. **Ask mentors for forwards, explicitly.** Professors/advisors get: what you're looking for (1 sentence) + best-work link + "would you introduce me to anyone relevant?" + a paste-ready blurb.

## ⛔ The Drafting Protocol — DO THIS BEFORE WRITING A WORD (non-negotiable)

This is where outreach most often fails: leading with the WRONG, WEAK, or UNVERIFIED finding. The résumé compresses results into one bullet — drafting from that bullet picks a weaker hook than the real work supports, and risks fabricating. Always:

1. **Inventory the candidate's real artifacts.** Check their public writing first — blogs, posts, repos (list yours in `~/.claude/profile.md`). A topical blog post that explains a finding beats a bare repo link.
2. **READ the specific artifact the hook rests on** — the actual blog post / paper section / repo README — NOT the résumé summary. Find the *strongest, most specific* real finding (numbers, mechanism, surprising result).
3. **Match finding → recipient.** Pick the result that connects to THIS person's work, not a one-size-fits-all line. (E.g. a constitution/synthetic-data result → a synthetic-data researcher; a probing/sharpening result → a probes researcher.) Different recipient, different finding from the same body of work.
4. **Verify every claim against the artifact.** Each number/finding in the email must trace to the source you just read. The Zen human-touch gate checks TONE, not FACTUAL ACCURACY — this step is the separate fact-check. Never cite a result you haven't confirmed in the artifact. Never claim a method/result that wasn't actually run.
5. THEN write to the voice rules below, and run the Zen human-touch gate.

**Why this is non-negotiable:** drafting from the résumé bullet has produced wrong-and-weak hooks (leading with a minor result while the candidate's best finding sat unused) and near-fabrications (claiming a method had no result when a blog post showed it won). Read the source, pick the strongest true finding, match it to the person.

## Structure

```
Subject: <anchor on shared context, not the ask>

Hi <Name>,

<One warm opening sentence + one factual sentence about why you're writing.>

<Quick context line — e.g., "Quick context on me:" or "Some background:">

- Bullet 1 — most credible/recent fact
- Bullet 2 — closest match to recipient's work
- Bullet 3 — specific build with metric or method
- Bullet 4 — recent/active project
- Bullet 5 (optional) — relevant prior

<One soft interest line — never two. State what you'd want to do, with low-stakes framing.>

<CTA that gives the recipient an easy out — let them forward instead of decide.>

Cheers,
<Name>
<email · LinkedIn · GitHub or other relevant link>
```

**Length target:** 120-180 words including signature. Anything longer = needs a cut.

## The 10 Voice Rules

1. **Subject line anchors on shared context, not the ask.**
   - Good: `Following up — Karthik (Daytona Meetup yesterday)`
   - Bad: `Resume for review` / `Job inquiry` / `Following up on our conversation`

2. **Opening = one warm sentence + one factual sentence.** No throat-clearing. No "I hope this finds you well."
   - Good: `Great chatting yesterday at the Daytona AI Researchers Meetup — sending my resume over as you suggested.`
   - Bad: `I hope this email finds you well. Thank you so much for taking the time to speak with me yesterday at the Daytona AI Researchers Meetup. As we discussed, I am writing to follow up...`

3. **Body = bullets, not prose.** 4-6 bullets max, one line each. Each bullet = one fact + one specific.

4. **Lead with credentials, end with closest match.** Education → research → recent build → relevant prior. The most relevant-to-recipient bullet should be in position 2 or 3 (top of skim zone).

5. **Keep specifics, drop superlatives.**
   - Keep: model names, metrics, methods, venues, dates
   - Drop: "passionate", "excited", "thrilled", "world-class", "cutting-edge", "impactful"

6. **One soft interest line — never two.**
   - Good: `The environments work at Bespoke is exactly the kind of stuff I'd love to dig into — happy to help out in whatever form makes sense.`
   - Bad (V7 had three of these stacked): `I have relevant experience... I'm especially interested in... would be glad to help out or contribute to that work in any capacity.`

7. **CTA gives the recipient an easy out.** Let them respond by forwarding, not by deciding.
   - Good: `Let me know if anything looks like a fit, or who I should be talking to.`
   - Bad: `I would love to schedule a 30-minute call to discuss potential opportunities at your earliest convenience.`

8. **Casual sign-off.** "Cheers," "Best," "Thanks," — not "Sincerely," "Best regards,"

9. **Don't read their tagline back to them.** They know what their company does. Mentioning "the Environments + Data Curation work at Bespoke" is fine; quoting their homepage is trying too hard.

10. **Tense + dates.** Use "yesterday" / "earlier today" / "last week" — concrete and natural. Avoid "recently", "in the past", "previously" — vague.

## Banned Phrases

Never use these. They signal formality, desperation, or filler:

- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "Thanks for taking the time" (use "Great chatting" or "Good to meet you" instead)
- "Reaching out with my profile" (just send the resume)
- "I would love to..." used more than once
- "Would be glad to..." → use "happy to..."
- "Quick snapshot" → use "Quick context on me" or "Some background"
- "Prior:" / "Previously:" → use "Earlier:"
- "At your earliest convenience"
- "Please let me know"
- "Looking forward to hearing from you"
- "Sincerely," / "Best regards,"
- "Per our conversation"
- "I am writing to..."

## What to Cut When the Draft Is Too Long

In order:
1. **Second interest line** — collapse to one
2. **Adjectives** — "successfully shipped" → "shipped", "deeply interested" → "interested"
3. **Throat-clearing in opening** — drop "Thanks for X, I really appreciated Y" → just one sentence
4. **CTA hedges** — "if that works for you" / "whenever convenient" → drop
5. **Weakest bullet** — the one least relevant to recipient
6. **Specifics in bullets** — keep model names + metrics, drop generic descriptors

## What to Add When the Draft Is Too Short / Generic

In order:
1. **Concrete metric or method on weakest bullet** — "fine-tuned models" → "GRPO fine-tuning on 4×H100"
2. **Venue / date for credentials** — "papers on X" → "papers on X (GECCO '25, ALife 2025)"
3. **Soft interest line** — if missing, add one (just one)
4. **Relevant project not yet mentioned** — only if it strengthens the closest-match angle

## Iteration Pattern

Show draft → user reviews → user gives directional feedback ("too formal", "drop X", "add Y") → revise → repeat until user says lock it.

Never iterate on more than one dimension per round (tone OR content OR length — not all three).

## Anti-Patterns

- ❌ Multi-paragraph prose body
- ❌ Restating the recipient's company tagline
- ❌ Listing every project the user has done
- ❌ Three-sentence opening
- ❌ Two soft interest lines
- ❌ Formal sign-off ("Sincerely")
- ❌ Adding emojis (unless user explicitly asks)
- ❌ Bullet points longer than one line each
- ❌ Buzzwords that mean nothing ("synergistic", "cutting-edge", "impactful")
- ❌ Begging energy ("would really appreciate", "any opportunity at all")

## After Sending — The Follow-Up Loop (rule 6; the part that actually converts)

Conversion lives in the 2nd and 3rd touch — *"the only failed outreach is the one-and-done."* A single draft is not the job; the cadence is. After the first message:

**The cadence (3 touches max, all short):**
- **Touch 1** — the initial message (above). Record: person, channel, artifact used, date sent, status.
- **Touch 2 — day 7–9 if no reply.** Two lines, re-link the artifact, zero guilt. ("Circling back — re-sending the [artifact] in case it slipped. Still very interested.") Compute the due date when you send touch 1.
- **Touch 3 — 3–4 weeks later, ONLY if the thread went alive-then-dead** (they replied once, then went silent). One line of new news ("since we last spoke I shipped/wrote X"). Don't touch-3 a never-replied thread.

**Reviving dead threads (rule 7):** old recruiters / stalled processes / months-silent interviews → one re-ping with a line of news. Dead threads convert; abandoned ones never do. (E.g. this session revived a Feb recruiter thread with a warm re-open.)

### Checking replies + queuing follow-ups (ON-DEMAND step — run when the user says "check my outreach")

This is a documented step, not built automation. When asked:
1. **Search Gmail for replies** to the outreach threads (`mcp__claude_ai_Gmail__search_threads` by recipient / subject; check each thread's latest message sender).
2. **Classify each thread:** replied (→ handle the reply, no follow-up needed) · no-reply past day 7–9 (→ draft touch-2) · alive-then-dead past 3–4 wk (→ draft touch-3) · still inside the window (→ wait).
3. **Draft the due follow-ups** per the cadence above, save as Gmail drafts, and update the tracker (touch_count, last_touch, next_touch_due, status).
4. Report: who replied, who's due for a follow-up (drafted), who's still in-window.

**Tracking:** every thread is logged so nothing silently expires — person, channel, handle, touch_count, last_touch, next_touch_due, status, artifact. The `find-and-reach` skill maintains `outreach/tracking_sheet.csv`; this is the single sheet. (Future: this loop can be automated to run on a schedule — for now it's run on request.)

## Pre-Flight (before any outreach campaign) — check the candidate's own history

Before targeting people at a company, cross-reference the candidate's application + outreach history (Gmail + the Job Applications Log):
- **Recently rejected at a team** (< ~1 month) → don't cold-outreach that team; it reads as pressure. Pick a different team or wait.
- **Live pending application** → outreach can help, but keep it soft/value-first; never "reconsider me."
- **Already a warm thread** (a recruiter who reached out, a prior interviewer) → revive that thread (rule 7) rather than starting cold.
This pre-flight prevents tone-deaf outreach to teams that just said no. (This session: caught Safeguards-Labs + Agents rejections and a live Alignment app — which reshaped the whole targeting.)
